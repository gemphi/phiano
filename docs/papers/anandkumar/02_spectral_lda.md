# Three words from one document: excess correlation and the condition

*Phient interpreted papers, 2 of 16 · 2026-01-26 · on: "A Spectral Algorithm for Latent Dirichlet Allocation" (Anandkumar, Foster, Hsu, Kakade, Liu, 2012, Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems 25; arXiv:1204.6703)*

In Phient's world, a word (w) is nothing but what it relates to, under what conditions (c). The Phical paradigm, X ⇒ Y, drives and composes Phient's Phasor Language Models: Phinet, Phiano, PhiTon. Phical stores no point: a word is 32–64 relations, each a complex phasor on a closed circle with a count; meaning is a side effect of pairs of words moving under a condition.

## 1. Our abstract

This is the second of sixteen papers in which Phient reads one paper by Anima Anandkumar's group through its own paradigm. The first paper was about the general machine: moments as tensors, whitening, power iteration. This one is the machine pointed at text. The paper asks a narrow question. If a document is a bag of words drawn from a mixture of topics, and the mixture itself is drawn from a Dirichlet prior, can the topics be recovered from nothing more than which words occur together, in pairs and in triples? The paper's answer is yes, with a proof, and with an algorithm that needs only two singular value decompositions on k × k matrices, where k is the number of topics.

We read it here because its raw material is the closest thing in her early work to Phient's own. The moments the paper builds are statistics of two and three words drawn from one document. A document is a condition. Three words drawn under it are three words related under c. The paper's whole difficulty is that the condition is a mixture and not a single topic, and its whole trick is a correction, subtracted from the raw moments, that removes the part of the correlation that the mixture alone explains. What is left it calls excess correlation. That name is the reason this paper is worth a Phient reading. Phical's channels are meant to hold exactly the part of a word's relations that a condition does not already explain.

Sections 2 to 5 state what the paper says, in its terms, with the formulas it gives and the one experiment it reports. Sections 6 to 10 put its objects into the Phient family: the one-hot word as a function, the topic as a channel, the corrected moment as an operator X ⇒ Y under c, the topic matrix on the sphere, and the recovery loop as the quest. Section 11 lists what Phient has measured that bears on any of this, all of it from the facts file, none of it a topic model. Section 12 names one experiment that would test the paper's idea inside phinet, with a control. Section 13 says where the analogy breaks. Section 14 is about the person and the year. Section 15 is what is left.

One limit is stated up front. Phient has run no topic model, no LDA, no ECA, and has never estimated a third moment from a corpus. Every number about Phient on this page was measured for another purpose. Where the paper's idea has not been tried in gemphi, this page says so.

## 2. What the paper claims, in its own terms

The paper's abstract, which we fetched from the NIPS proceedings page and from the PDF, says the following. Topic modeling generalises clustering: observations, the words in a document, are generated by several latent factors at once, the topics, rather than by just one. That extra power makes the unsupervised learning problem harder, because only the words are seen and the topics are hidden. The paper gives what it calls a simple and efficient learning procedure that is guaranteed to recover the parameters for a wide class of topic models, including Latent Dirichlet Allocation. For LDA, the procedure recovers both the topic-word distributions and the parameters of the Dirichlet prior over the topic mixtures, using only trigram statistics, meaning third order moments that can be estimated from documents that contain just three words. The method is called Excess Correlation Analysis, ECA. It rests on a spectral decomposition of low order moments done with two singular value decompositions. The SVDs are carried out only on k × k matrices, where k is the number of topics, which is why the paper calls it scalable.

The authors are Anima Anandkumar, then at the University of California, Irvine; Dean P. Foster, University of Pennsylvania; Daniel Hsu and Sham M. Kakade, both at Microsoft Research in Cambridge, Massachusetts; and Yi-Kai Liu at the National Institute of Standards and Technology. The paper appeared in Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems 25 in 2012. The arXiv id given in the brief, 1204.6703, is correct and is the one used. The arXiv text is a longer version, version 4 of which is 37 pages with appendices. The fetched arXiv text says an earlier version carried the title "Two SVDs Suffice: Spectral decompositions for probabilistic topic modeling and latent Dirichlet allocation". That earlier title is the claim in five words.

The paper has four sections and an appendix: an introduction; the independent latent factors and LDA models; Excess Correlation Analysis; a discussion; and Appendix A, illustrative empirical results. The arXiv version adds a sample complexity theorem. The NIPS version numbers the LDA algorithm as Algorithm 2 and the LDA theorem as Theorem 3.2; the arXiv version numbers them Algorithm 3 and Theorem 4.3. This page uses the NIPS numbering.

The paper places itself against three lines of prior work. For a single topic per document, it credits Papadimitriou and co-authors in 2000 with the first recovery guarantees. Against Arora and co-authors in 2012, whose method needs anchor words that occur in one topic only, it says it relaxes that separation condition. Against earlier spectral methods that used eigenvector decompositions, citing Mossel and Roch in 2006 and Hsu, Kakade and Zhang in 2009, it says two SVDs suffice.

## 3. The mathematics, plainly

The setup. There are d words in the vocabulary and k topics. Each topic is a probability vector over words, and these k vectors are the columns of a d × k matrix O. A document has a hidden topic mixture h, a probability vector over the k topics. Each word in the document is drawn by first picking a topic from h and then a word from that topic's column. A word is written one-hot, as a vector in ℝ^d with a single 1. Then E[x_v | h] = O h. The one assumption the paper needs is Condition 2.1: O has full column rank.

Two models are treated. In the independent latent factor model, the coordinates of h are independent, each with a variance σ_i² and a third central moment μ_{i,3}. In LDA, h is drawn from a Dirichlet with parameter α ∈ ℝ^k, all entries positive, and α₀ = Σ_i α_i. The paper says knowing α₀ is a much weaker requirement than knowing all of α.

The moments. Let μ = E[x₁]. For the independent model the paper centres everything: Pairs = E[(x₁ − μ) ⊗ (x₂ − μ)], and Triples(η) is the d × d matrix E[(x₁ − μ)(x₂ − μ)ᵀ ⟨η, x₃ − μ⟩] for a probe vector η. Lemma 3.1 says Pairs = O diag(σ₁², …, σ_k²) Oᵀ and Triples(η) = O diag(Oᵀη) diag(μ_{1,3}, …, μ_{k,3}) Oᵀ.

For LDA the coordinates of h sum to one and are not independent, so the paper replaces centring by a correction that depends on α₀:

Pairs_{α₀} = E[x₁x₂ᵀ] − (α₀ / (α₀+1)) μμᵀ

Triples_{α₀}(η) = E[x₁x₂ᵀ⟨η, x₃⟩] − (α₀ / (α₀+2)) [E[x₁x₂ᵀ] η μᵀ + μ ηᵀ E[x₁x₂ᵀ] + ⟨η, μ⟩ E[x₁x₂ᵀ]] + (2α₀² / ((α₀+2)(α₀+1))) ⟨η, μ⟩ μμᵀ

Lemma 3.2 then gives the same shape as before: Pairs_{α₀} = (1 / ((α₀+1)α₀)) O diag(α) Oᵀ and Triples_{α₀}(η) = (2 / ((α₀+2)(α₀+1)α₀)) O diag(Oᵀη) diag(α) Oᵀ.

The algorithm. ECA, Algorithm 1, takes a vector θ ∈ ℝ^k and the two moments. Step one, find U ∈ ℝ^{d×k} whose range is the range of Pairs. Step two, find V ∈ ℝ^{k×k} so that Vᵀ(UᵀPairs U)V is the identity, and set W = UV. Step three, take the SVD of Wᵀ Triples(Wθ) W and keep the left singular vectors Ξ with non-repeated singular values. Step four, return {(W⁺)ᵀ ξ : ξ ∈ Ξ}, where A⁺ = (AᵀA)⁻¹Aᵀ. Algorithm 2, for LDA, runs the same three steps on the modified moments and then normalises each recovered column to sum to one.

The theorem. Theorem 3.2 says three things under Condition 2.1. No false positives: for every θ, Algorithm 2 returns a subset of the columns of O. Topic recovery: if θ is uniform on the unit sphere S^{k−1}, then with probability 1 it returns all the columns. Parameter recovery: α = α₀(α₀+1) (O⁺)ᵀ Pairs_{α₀} O⁺ 1, with 1 the vector of ones.

## 4. Its experiments and numbers as reported

The paper is almost entirely theory. Its one piece of data work is Appendix A, which the paper itself calls illustrative empirical results. The authors ran a variant of Algorithm 2 on the UCI "Bag of Words" dataset, the New York Times part of it: 300,000 articles, a vocabulary of d = 102,660 words, with k = 50 topics and α₀ = 0. They used a power iteration to obtain singular vectors rather than a single random θ. The moments were formed from word co-occurrence statistics across the documents. The result they show is a table of the top 25 words for each topic. The fetched text describes the topics as spanning sports, politics, finance, health and entertainment. There is no numerical accuracy metric, no held-out likelihood, no comparison against variational inference or Gibbs sampling, and no timing. The paper does not claim any of these. We do not supply them.

The arXiv version carries one more number, and it is a theorem rather than a measurement. Theorem 4.1 in that version, sample complexity for LDA, says there are universal constants C₁ and C₂ such that the following holds. Let p_min = min_i α_i / α₀ and let σ_k(O) be the smallest non-zero singular value of O. If N ≥ C₁ · ((α₀+1) / (p_min σ_k(O)²))² independent samples of (x₁, x₂, x₃) are used to form empirical moments, then with high probability the plug-in variant of Algorithm 2 returns columns Õ_i such that, for some permutation σ of [k], ‖O_i − Õ_{σ(i)}‖₂ ≤ C₂ · (α₀+1)² k³ / (p_min² σ_k(O)³ √N) for every i. The bound has no dependence on d, the vocabulary size. It depends on k cubed, on how uneven the Dirichlet is through p_min, on how well conditioned the topics are through σ_k(O), and on α₀. The constants C₁ and C₂ are not given as numbers.

Three things should be stated plainly. First, the experiment's α₀ = 0 is the single-topic limit; the paper says that as α₀ → 0 the modified moments tend to the raw cross moments E[x₁ ⊗ x₂] and E[x₁ ⊗ x₂ ⊗ x₃]. So the one run reported is at the easy end of the model. Second, the experiment used power iteration, which the discussion says is significantly more stable than the single-θ SVD of Algorithm 1. The reported words come from a cousin of the procedure proved. Third, the appendix calls itself illustrative. The strength of the paper is the theorem, with an explicit rate in N, an explicit power of k, and no vocabulary term.

## 5. The one idea, restated

Strip the paper to one sentence and it is this: the correlation between two words in the same document, once you subtract what a mixture of topics would produce on its own, is a matrix of rank k whose factors are the topics, and the same subtraction on three words gives a tensor whose slices share those factors and are told apart by a probe.

The word "excess" is doing the work. Two words from the same document are correlated for two reasons. One is that they came from the same mixture h, and a mixture that leans toward one topic makes both words lean that way. The other is that within a topic, some words simply co-occur. The paper's correction, the α₀ / (α₀+1) times μμᵀ term and its three-word cousin, is the amount of correlation that the first reason explains under the Dirichlet prior. What is left, the excess, is O diag(α) Oᵀ up to a scalar. That is the topic matrix and nothing else.

The second half of the idea is the probe. Pairs alone gives you the span of the topics, the k-dimensional subspace they live in, but not the individual columns, because O diag(α) Oᵀ has the same value for any rotation of O within that span. Triples(η), contracted with a random vector η, gives a matrix whose singular values are, after whitening, the numbers ⟨O_i, η⟩ up to scale. A random η makes those numbers distinct with probability 1, and distinct singular values pin down the singular vectors, which are the whitened topics. That is why one SVD whitens and the second SVD separates. The discussion section says the first SVD is based on correlation between two variables and the second finds directions exhibiting non-Gaussianity. The method is the same shape as independent component analysis with the third cumulant standing in for kurtosis.

In Phient's vocabulary the sentence is shorter. A document is a condition c. Two words seen under c carry a correlation that is partly c's and partly theirs. Remove c's share and what remains is the words' relation. A third word under the same c, contracted with a probe, tells the relations apart. The paper is a proof that this can be done with two matrices of size k × k, and a bound on how many documents it takes.

## 6. A word as a function

The paper writes a word as a one-hot vector in ℝ^d. This is the flattest function a word can be: a delta on the vocabulary, value 1 at itself and 0 elsewhere. Every structure the paper finds lives in expectations of these deltas, never in the deltas themselves. The expectation E[x₁ | h] = O h is the first place a word becomes a function of something, and the something is the hidden mixture. Given h, a word is a distribution over the vocabulary, a column-mixture of O.

Phical begins one step later. It stores no point for a word, and so no delta. A word in phinet is a Tensor of shape [b, w, s], complex, where the s axis holds the word's relations: 32–64 channels, each a phasor on a closed circle with a count. The paper's word after conditioning, O h, is a real vector of length d with entries that sum to one. Phical's word is a complex vector of length 64 with entries on the unit circle. These are different objects and the page does not pretend they are the same. What they share is the move from a point to a function. In the paper the function is over the vocabulary. In Phical the function is over relations.

There is a way to read the paper's O that makes the two closer. A column of O is a topic, a function on words. A row of O is a word, a function on topics: for each topic, how much the word belongs to it. If k were 64, a row of O would have the shape of a Phical word, real rather than complex, with weights rather than phases. The paper's theorems are about columns, never rows. But Lemma 3.2 says Pairs_{α₀} = (1 / ((α₀+1)α₀)) O diag(α) Oᵀ, and the (u, v) entry of that matrix is the inner product of row u and row v of O, weighted by α. Two words' excess correlation is the inner product of their functions over topics. That is a relation between two words computed from their functions, which is the Phical sentence.

The difference that matters is the count. A Phical channel carries a phase and a count. A row of O carries a weight and nothing else. The paper puts the count elsewhere: it is N, the number of documents, and it appears only in the sample complexity bound, as 1/√N. Phical keeps the count next to the phase and uses it during training. The paper averages over N first and then decomposes. This is the first place the two paradigms part, and Section 13 comes back to it.

## 7. The relation channel as a coordinate

The paper's central object after whitening is W, a d × k matrix such that Wᵀ Pairs_{α₀} W is the identity. In W's coordinates, the k topics are orthonormal. The second SVD then finds those orthonormal directions one by one. Each direction is a topic, and a topic in whitened space is a coordinate: a unit vector along which every word has a scalar value.

Phical's channel is also a coordinate, but it is chosen in advance. A word has 64 channels, and channel j means the same relation for every word: the j-th coordinate of the relation circle. The paper finds its coordinates from the data and proves they are the right ones. Phical fixes its coordinates and asks the training to fill them. The first paper in this series said the same about the general tensor method. This paper sharpens it, because here the coordinates have a name. Each one is a topic, a probability vector over words, and the paper recovers it exactly in the population limit.

So the question this paper puts to Phical is direct. Is a channel a topic? Nothing in gemphi has been measured that would say. The facts file records that the 64 channels are indexed as spherical harmonics Y_l^m with l < 8 in the sphere tie, as 64 hexagram gates, and as base-4 ACGT codons in the helix. Each is a coordinate system imposed from outside. None was learned from a corpus by a decomposition. The quest oracle gives channels a meaning by fiat: kinds is-a, part-of, near, used-for, unlike, families of 8 over 256 words. There the channel is the relation kind, and the measured wrong-channel MRR of 0.015 against within-kind 0.747 says the channels do hold kind apart once trained. The coordinate was given, not found.

The paper offers a way to make the comparison exact. Project every word's one-hot vector onto the whitened topics W. Each word gets k real coordinates along k learned directions. A Phical word's channels are 64 values along 64 fixed directions. If one built Pairs_{α₀} from the Austen corpus the operator was trained on, whitened it, and asked whether the learned directions line up with any of the 64 channels, one would have a measurement. It has not been done. Section 12 says how it could be.

What the paper does not have is the circle. A whitened topic is a direction in ℝ^k, and a word's value along it is a signed real number. Phical's value along a channel is a phase, a point on a closed circle of 256 quanta. The paper's coordinate is a line. Phical's coordinate is a loop. Whether a loop is the better coordinate for language is the wager of the whole project and this paper does not settle it either way.

## 8. The operator as X ⇒ Y under c

The paper has no operator in the neural sense, no learned map from a function to a function. What it has is a fixed linear map, built from moments, whose structure is the theorem. Triples_{α₀}(η) is a d × d matrix, and it is linear in η. So η ↦ Triples_{α₀}(η) is a map from a probe vector to a matrix. After whitening it becomes θ ↦ Wᵀ Triples_{α₀}(Wθ) W, a map from ℝ^k to k × k matrices. Lemma 3.2 says this map has a closed form: up to a scalar, it is θ ↦ diag(Oᵀ W θ) in the whitened basis. The probe picks a scalar for each topic and the matrix is diagonal in the topic basis with those scalars on the diagonal.

Read that as X ⇒ Y under c. X is the pair (x₁, x₂), two words. Y is the matrix of their excess correlation. c is the third word x₃, contracted against a probe. The paper's third-moment tensor, sliced along its third axis by η, is a family of operators on pairs of words, indexed by a condition. That is the Phical sentence, and it is the reason the brief chose this paper for Phient.

In phinet the operator is Fno: a unitary FFT over the channel axis, learned complex weights on the retained modes, an inverse FFT, a local path, a nonlinearity. It is trained to move one word's function toward the next word's function. The facts file gives its numbers on Austen: identity 7.9× chance MRR, hinge with 8,360 parameters 14.7× valid and 14.6× test, softmax at β=8 18.0× and 18.8×, softmax wide and deep with 50,016 parameters 27.6× at 3 epochs and 30.7× valid, 30.3× test at 8 epochs, hit@10 0.348. The pool was 1,228 words seen 20 or more times, 204,592 training examples, chance MRR 0.0063. Those numbers describe an operator that takes a word and returns a ranking over next words. The paper's operator takes two words and a probe and returns a correlation. They are not the same map.

But they are made of the same material. Phinet's operator is a multiplication in the frequency domain. The paper's operator, in the whitened basis, is a multiplication by a diagonal. The paper's whitening finds the basis in which the third moment's slices are simultaneously diagonal. Phinet's FFT assumes that basis is the Fourier basis on the channel circle. The paper proves its basis is correct for LDA. Phinet has 62/62 parity tests against a PyTorch reference that say its FFT does what an FFT does, and 30.7× chance that says the Fourier basis is useful for next-word ranking. Neither is a proof that the Fourier basis diagonalises anything about Austen. This paper is the one that makes that objection sharp.

## 9. The sphere, the tensor, and the probe

Theorem 3.2's topic recovery clause draws θ uniformly from the unit sphere S^{k−1}. The sphere appears in the paper for one reason: a random direction has, with probability 1, distinct inner products with each of k fixed vectors, and distinct singular values are what make singular vectors unique. The sphere is a source of generic probes and nothing more. The paper does not put any structure on it.

Phient's sphere is different in kind. The facts file describes phinet::Tie::Sphere: one complex weight per spherical-harmonic degree, with 64 = 8² channels indexed as Y_l^m with l < 8. There the sphere is the domain the word's function lives on, and its harmonics are the channels. The measured numbers are 1,192 parameters, Austen hinge at 3 epochs 12.8× valid and 13.1× test, hit@10 0.182, 89 seconds, against the circle's 14.7× and 14.6× with 8,360 parameters in 142 seconds. On a synthetic degree-filter task of 200 items over 40 epochs the circle reached MRR 0.662 with identity at 0.646, and the sphere reached 1.000. So Phient's sphere is a place where functions live and are filtered by degree, and the paper's sphere is a place random vectors are drawn from. The two should not be confused, and this page does not build a bridge that is not there.

The tensor is the closer match. Triples_{α₀} is a symmetric d × d × d tensor, and Lemma 3.2 says it is a sum of k rank-one terms O_i ⊗ O_i ⊗ O_i weighted by α_i, up to a scalar. That is the moment tensor from the first paper in this series, with a correction that makes it hold under the Dirichlet. Phinet's Tensor is [b, w, s] complex, a batch of words by channels. It is a container, not a moment. Phinet has never formed a third moment of anything. The nearest structure in gemphi is HRR, which the facts file lists among phinet's parts. HRR binds two vectors by circular convolution, an elementwise product in the Fourier basis. Triples(η) in the whitened basis is diagonal. Both are pointwise products in a chosen basis. That is a formal similarity and it stops there; nothing measured connects them.

The probe is the piece Phient lacks entirely. The paper needs η only to break ties, but the fact that one random vector, contracted against the third axis, separates all k topics is the paper's cleverest step. Phical's condition c selects which relations move. In the quest it is the relation kind. In the operator on Austen it is the previous word. Neither is a random probe. The paper's lesson is that the probe need not be meaningful; it need only be generic. Whether a generic condition would separate Phical's channels the way a generic η separates topics is a question Section 12 turns into an experiment.

## 10. The recovery loop as the quest

The paper's Algorithm 1 is not a loop. It is four steps run once: reduce, whiten, SVD, reconstruct. The appendix says the authors used a power iteration to obtain the singular vectors, and the discussion calls tensor power iteration significantly more stable than Algorithm 1. So the paper as proved is one shot, and the paper as run is iterative. The first paper in this series treated power iteration at length. This page takes the one-shot version at its word.

The quest in phiecs is the loop that most resembles a recovery procedure. The facts file describes it: a synthetic oracle of 256 words in families of 8, with kinds is-a, part-of, near, used-for, unlike; 1,280 rows; 293 target words; chance MRR 0.021. The shipped trainer reached 0.082 MRR. Attraction-only training with dithered rounding reached 0.594 MRR, hit@1 34.8%, hit@5 97.4%. Within-kind MRR was 0.747, wrong channel 0.015, offset reading 0.005. On a held-out 20%, one hop gave 0.011; two hops gave is-a 0.095, used-for 0.093, and part-of, near and unlike about chance.

The quest and ECA are trying to do the same job from opposite directions. ECA is given the moments of the whole corpus at once and solves for the structure in closed form. The quest is given one row at a time and nudges phasors toward agreement. ECA's guarantee is exact recovery in the population limit and a 1/√N rate in samples. The quest's measured result is 0.594 MRR against 0.021 chance, with no rate and no proof. ECA's failure mode is conditioning: the bound has σ_k(O)³ in the denominator, and near-parallel topics blow it up. The quest's measured failure mode is generalisation: one hop held out gives 0.011, which is below chance, and only is-a and used-for survive two hops. These are different failures and neither method's strength speaks to the other's weakness.

There is one point of genuine contact. ECA's no-false-positives clause says that whatever θ you choose, the algorithm never returns a vector that is not a column of O. It may miss topics, but it never invents one. The quest's wrong-channel number, 0.015, is the nearest Phient has to a no-false-positives measurement: asked to read a relation from the wrong channel, the trained lexicon returns nearly nothing. That is measured, not proved, and on a synthetic oracle with five kinds, not on text. But it is the same shape of claim. The recovered structure lives in its own coordinates and does not leak into others.

Where the paper's loop and Phient's part is the helix. The facts file describes the lexicon as a DNA-coded tree, 64 codons as base-4 ACGT of channel, 18,496 words in 1.9 seconds, 75 MB, byte-exact round trip. ECA has no such artefact. Its output is a d × k matrix and a k-vector α, and the paper says nothing about storing or reusing them.

The persona rows deserve a sentence. The paper's h is a document's mixture over topics. Phient's nearest thing to a document with a mixture is a held-out four-sentence paragraph from Chesterton, Austen or Carroll, and the task is to say which writer. That is the α₀ → 0 limit, one topic per document, and the 71% is a classification accuracy, not a parameter recovery.

The decoding numbers do not bear on the paper and are listed only to say so. Trigram sampler Kneser-Ney perplexity 13.3, operator nearest-k 66.9 (was 97.2), union 66.8; shared 4-grams across prompts 25.7% for the sampler and 8.7% for the operator. The paper's trigram statistics are counts of three words in one document without regard to order. A Kneser-Ney trigram is an ordered count of three adjacent words. The word "trigram" appears in both and means different things.

The byte deadband bears in a way the paper cannot. A channel is one byte, 256 quanta of 2π; a nudge of 0.06·sin d falls under half a quantum once sin d is below 0.2, about 11 degrees; nearest rounding froze every pair 8 quanta short; dither costs corpus attribution 71% to 65% and ships off by default. ECA works in floating point and has no deadband. Any attempt to run its ideas inside Phical's byte channels would meet the deadband first.

The engine number is the last: allocation and bandwidth-bound at these sizes, a 1 MB op in 2 to 5 ms. Pairs_{α₀} for the Austen pool would be a 1,228 × 1,228 real matrix, about 12 MB in f64, and Triples(η) for one η is another of the same size. The paper's method at Phient's scale is not blocked by the engine. It is simply unrun.

## 12. The experiment that would test the paper's idea inside phinet

The experiment is to run ECA on the same Austen corpus the operator was trained on and ask whether the directions it finds are related to Phical's channels. Every piece of it can be done with what exists, plus a whitening and two SVDs that gemphi does not yet have.

Corpus and pool. Use the three Austen novels, Emma, Persuasion, Sense and Sensibility, and the 1,228-word pool of words seen 20 or more times, as in the facts file. Define a document as a sentence, or as a fixed window, and record which choice. Draw triples of distinct words from each document. Estimate E[x₁x₂ᵀ] and E[x₁x₂ᵀ⟨η, x₃⟩] as empirical averages over documents, for one fixed random η, and μ as the empirical word frequency. Set k to a small number to start, say 16, and try α₀ at 0 and at 1, since the paper's own experiment used 0 and its method is meant for larger values.

The procedure. Form Pairs_{α₀}, take its top-k range U, whiten to W, form Wᵀ Triples_{α₀}(Wθ) W for θ uniform on the sphere, SVD, reconstruct, normalise. This is Algorithm 2 verbatim. Report the k columns and their top 25 words, as the paper's appendix does, so the output can be read by eye.

The measurement. Project every pool word's one-hot vector onto the whitened basis W to get k real coordinates per word. Take the same word's 64 Phical channels from the trained lexicon. Compute, for each of the k ECA directions and each of the 64 channels, the circular-linear correlation between the ECA coordinate and the channel's phase across the 1,228 words. Report the largest such correlation for each ECA direction, and the distribution over all 64 × k pairs.

The control. Replace the trained lexicon with an untrained one, phases drawn uniformly on the circle, counts zero, and repeat the correlation. The control tells you how large a correlation arises by chance between k real coordinates and 64 random phases across 1,228 words. If the trained lexicon's largest correlations are not distinguishable from the control's, then ECA's directions and Phical's channels are unrelated on Austen, and that is the result. A cheaper second control is to shuffle the channel phases across words and recompute.

A second measurement, closer to the paper's own claim. Use the ECA topics as a ranker: for a prompt word u, score each candidate v by the (u, v) entry of Pairs_{α₀} reconstructed from the recovered factors. Report MRR on the same held-out ranking task the operator was measured on, against chance 0.0063. Its number is not known and no guess is offered. If it lands anywhere near 30.7× chance then much of what the operator learned is pair correlation of rank k. If it lands near identity's 7.9×, the operator is doing something the moments do not capture.

Report both numbers, the control, the choice of document, k, α₀, and the wall time. Nothing here has been run.

## 13. Where we disagree or where the analogy breaks

The analogy breaks in four places, and the page states each.

First, the model. LDA is a bag of words. The paper's exchangeability assumption says x₁, x₂, x₃ are exchangeable given h, and its whole apparatus depends on it: the moments are symmetric because word order does not matter. Phical is built on the opposite premise. Its operator on Austen is trained to move one word toward the next word, and the facts file's decoding measurements are about generated sequences. The quest's held-out numbers are about hops. Order is the condition in Phical, or a large part of it. The paper's model discards order on purpose, and so its trigram is not Phical's trigram, and its condition, the document, is not Phical's condition.

Second, the count. Section 6 said it and it bears repeating. The paper averages over N documents first and then decomposes. Its count is a single number that enters the error bound as 1/√N. Phical keeps a count per channel per word and uses it during training. The byte deadband is a direct consequence: a count-weighted nudge below half a quantum does nothing. ECA has no analogue and no need for one. Whether Phical's per-channel count is a strength or an accident is not settled by this paper, but the paper shows that a moment method can do without it.

Third, the basis. The paper finds its coordinates and proves they are the topics. Phical fixes 64 coordinates on a circle and trains into them. The synthetic degree-filter result, circle 0.662 against sphere 1.000, is a measured case where the fixed basis mattered by a large factor on a task built to favour one of them. It says nothing about Austen. Phient has one measurement showing a basis can matter and none showing which basis Austen prefers. The paper's method is a way to find out.

Fourth, the guarantee. The paper's result is a theorem with a rate. Phient's results are measurements with controls. The page does not rank them. But the paper's bound has k³ and σ_k(O)³ in it. For k = 64 on a vocabulary of 1,228, k³ is 262,144 and the bound is likely vacuous at any N Phient could gather from three novels. The paper's own experiment used k = 50 on 300,000 documents. Phient's operator was trained on 204,592 examples from a 1,228-word pool. The regimes differ and the paper's guarantee is not one Phient could invoke.

One disagreement of taste, stated as such. The paper's α₀ correction is exact for the Dirichlet and for nothing else. The elegance of Lemma 3.2 depends on the Dirichlet's moments having the specific form that lets the μμᵀ term cancel. Phical makes no distributional assumption about its condition. That is either a freedom or a lack of discipline, and the facts file has no number that decides it.

## 14. The person and the moment

Everything in this section is from the profile in this folder and nothing else.

The paper is dated 2012. The profile's table puts her at the University of California, Irvine from 2010 to 2018, assistant and then tenured associate professor, working on large-scale machine learning, high-dimensional statistics, and tensor decompositions for latent-variable models. The PDF's affiliation line agrees: Irvine. So this is a paper from the second year of her first faculty position, written with two collaborators at Microsoft Research and one each at Pennsylvania and NIST. The profile says her first decade was tensor methods, the recognition that the moments of a latent-variable model form a tensor whose decomposition recovers the model, with guarantees. This paper is that sentence applied to text, one year before the NSF CAREER Award the profile lists for 2013 and two before the Sloan Fellowship in 2014.

The profile's account of her training explains the paper's shape. A B.Tech. in electrical engineering at IIT Madras, a Ph.D. at Cornell on scalable algorithms for distributed statistical inference under Lang Tong, and a postdoc in Alan Willsky's Stochastic Systems Group at MIT. The paper's method is the method of moments, which is an estimation idea older than any of the machine learning around it, and the paper's discipline is that of a statistician: state the model, derive the moments, prove the recovery, bound the sample size. The discussion's line about the second SVD finding directions exhibiting non-Gaussianity is a signal-processing sentence. The profile says nothing about this paper directly and this page adds nothing the profile does not support.

The moment matters for what came after. The profile says a neural network maps vectors to vectors while a neural operator maps functions to functions, and it credits her group's Fourier Neural Operator in 2020 with doing the global integral kernel as a multiplication in the frequency domain. The 2012 paper already does its hard step as a multiplication in a chosen basis: whiten, then the third moment is diagonal. What changed between 2012 and 2020 was not the shape of the idea but who chooses the basis. In 2012 the basis is found from the data and proved correct. In 2020 the basis is fixed as Fourier and the weights on it are learned. Phinet follows the 2020 choice. This page has argued that the 2012 choice is the one that asks the harder question of Phical.

The profile's two honest notes apply here unchanged. Phient has done no weather work, and nothing here should be read as her endorsement. No partnership is agreed with anyone. She campaigned, the profile says, to rename the conference this paper appeared at. This page uses the name the proceedings used in 2012 because the venue line does.

## 15. What remains

What remains is mostly the experiment in Section 12, which has not been run, and three questions it would not answer even if it were.

The experiment would say whether ECA's directions on Austen line up with Phical's channels, and whether a rank-k pair correlation is a competitive next-word ranker on the operator's own task. Both are measurable with what exists plus a whitening and two SVDs. The wall time is not a concern; Section 11 said the matrices are about 12 MB each and the engine handles that. What is needed is the code, the choice of document, and the discipline to run the control first. None of it is done.

The first open question is order. ECA's moments are symmetric because LDA is a bag of words. Phical's condition is, on Austen, the previous word, which is ordered. A version of the paper's method for ordered triples would use E[x₁ ⊗ x₂ ⊗ x₃] with the three positions distinguished, which the paper's Lemma 3.2 does not cover and its symmetric SVD step would not apply to. The first paper in this series treated the asymmetric case through the general tensor method. Whether an ordered excess-correlation tensor on Austen has low rank is unknown and is the more interesting question for Phient, because it is the one Phical's operator is implicitly betting on.

The second open question is the count. The paper's error bound goes as 1/√N. Phient has no learning curve in N on any task. Running the operator on Austen at N = 25,000, 50,000, 100,000 and 204,592 examples and plotting MRR against √N would give Phient its first rate. It would also say whether the operator's 30.7× is near saturation at the pool's size or still climbing. That is a measurement the paper's theorem invites and Phient has not made.

The third open question is the deadband. ECA works in floating point and has no deadband. If a channel is one byte and a nudge below half a quantum does nothing, then any moment-based estimate stored into Phical's channels would be quantised to 256 levels on the circle. The paper's error bound assumes real arithmetic. What the bound becomes under byte quantisation is a question about Phical and not about the paper, and it is unanswered. The facts file's dither result, attribution 71% to 65% with dither on, says the quantisation is not free to fix.

What is not open is the paper's own claim. Two SVDs on k × k matrices recover LDA's topics and Dirichlet parameters from trigram statistics, with a proof and a rate. Phient does not dispute it, has not tested it, and has no number that touches it. The paper's value to Phient is the question it asks: given the data, what basis would a decomposition choose, and is it the one Phical fixed? That question is measurable and has not been measured. It should be.

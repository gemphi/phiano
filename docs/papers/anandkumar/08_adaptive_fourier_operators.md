# Mixing in the frequency domain: the shrunk mode and the byte deadband

*Phient interpreted papers, 8 of 16 · 2026-04-20 · on: "Adaptive Fourier Neural Operators: Efficient Token Mixers for Transformers" (Guibas, Mardani, Li, Tao, Anandkumar, Catanzaro, 2022, ICLR 2022; arXiv:2111.13587)*

In Phient's world, a word (w) is nothing but what it relates to, under what conditions (c). The Phical paradigm, X ⇒ Y, drives and composes Phient's Phasor Language Models: Phinet, Phiano, PhiTon. Phical stores no point: a word is 32–64 relations, each a complex phasor on a closed circle with a count; meaning is a side effect of pairs of words moving under a condition.

## 1. Our abstract

This is the eighth of sixteen papers in which Phient reads one paper by Anima Anandkumar's group through its own paradigm. The paper is the one in which the Fourier Neural Operator left partial differential equations and went into a vision transformer. Its claim is narrow and practical. Self-attention mixes tokens at a cost that grows with the square of the number of tokens. A global convolution done in the Fourier domain mixes them at a cost that grows as N log N. The FNO already does that, but with a full channel-mixing matrix at every retained frequency, so its parameter count grows with resolution and its weights are the same whatever the input. The paper makes three changes: it cuts the channel matrix into k diagonal blocks, it replaces the per-frequency weights with one small MLP shared across all frequencies, and it shrinks small Fourier coefficients to zero with a soft threshold. It calls the result the Adaptive Fourier Neural Operator, AFNO, and it reports that AFNO matches self-attention on inpainting and few-shot segmentation at fewer FLOPs, and beats the other efficient mixers it tried on Cityscapes at 1024 × 1024, a sequence of 65k tokens.

We read it here because it is the paper closest to what phinet actually is. phinet has a unitary FFT, an Fno with learned complex weights on retained modes, and a Tape for autograd. Every one of the paper's three changes is a question that can be put to that code. Block-diagonal channel mixing asks whether Phical's 64 channels should mix freely or in groups, and the sphere tie, one complex weight per spherical-harmonic degree, is already a grouped answer. Weight sharing across frequencies asks whether the operator should be the same at every mode, which is what a tie is. Soft-thresholding asks what to do with small coefficients, and Phient has an unplanned answer in the byte deadband, where a nudge smaller than half a quantum is lost.

Sections 2 to 5 state what the paper says, with its numbers as reported. Sections 6 to 10 place the paper's objects inside the Phient family. Section 11 lists what Phient has measured that bears on it, from the facts file only. Section 12 names the experiment, with its control. Section 13 says where the analogy breaks, and it breaks on the first axis: the paper's N is the number of pixels and is large, while Phical's function has 32–64 coordinates and is small. Section 14 is about the author and the winter of 2021. Section 15 is what remains.

One limit is stated up front. Nothing in gemphi is a vision model, no image has been passed through phinet, and phinet's Fno has never been run with a soft threshold, a block structure or a shared MLP. What follows is a reading, and the numbers about Phient below were all measured for other reasons.

## 2. What the paper claims, in its own terms

The paper's abstract says what it is for in its first three sentences. "Vision transformers have delivered tremendous success in representation learning. This is primarily due to effective token mixing through self attention. However, this scales quadratically with the number of pixels, which becomes infeasible for high-resolution inputs." The proposal follows. "To cope with this challenge, we propose Adaptive Fourier Neural Operator (AFNO) as an efficient token mixer that learns to mix in the Fourier domain."

The justification is that operator learning gives a principled way to see token mixing. "AFNO is based on a principled foundation of operator learning which allows us to frame token mixing as a continuous global convolution without any dependence on the input resolution. This principle was previously used to design FNO, which solves global convolution efficiently in the Fourier domain and has shown promise in learning challenging PDEs."

Then the changes. "To handle challenges in visual representation learning such as discontinuities in images and high resolution inputs, we propose principled architectural modifications to FNO which results in memory and computational efficiency. This includes imposing a block-diagonal structure on the channel mixing weights, adaptively sharing weights across tokens, and sparsifying the frequency modes via soft-thresholding and shrinkage."

And the result. "The resulting model is highly parallel with a quasi-linear complexity and has linear memory in the sequence size. AFNO outperforms self-attention mechanisms for few-shot segmentation in terms of both efficiency and accuracy. For Cityscapes segmentation with the Segformer-B3 backbone, AFNO can handle a sequence size of 65k and outperforms other efficient self-attention mechanisms."

The paper's own account of why the plain FNO is not enough for images is in three sentences spread over section 4. "The channel mixing in standard FNO incurs a quadratic complexity in the channel size." "The parameter count is however O(Nd²) as each token has its own channel mixing weights, which poorly scales with the image resolution." "The weights W_m,n are static, which can negatively impact the generalization." Each change answers one sentence. Blocks answer the first. The shared MLP answers the second and third at once. The soft threshold answers a fourth observation the paper makes about its data: "Images are inherently sparse in the Fourier domain, and most of the energy is concentrated around low frequency modes."

The conclusion restates the claim without adding to it. "We leverage the geometric structure of images in order to build an efficient token mixer in comparison to self-attention. Inspired by global convolution, we borrow Fourier Neural Operators (FNO) from PDEs for mixing tokens and propose principled architectural modifications to adapt FNO for images." The paper names one direction for later work, "exploring alternatives for the DFT such as the Wavelet transform to better capture locality," and has no limitations section. The paper was published as a conference paper at ICLR 2022; the arXiv preprint was submitted on 24 November 2021. The affiliations on the PDF are NVIDIA, the California Institute of Technology and Stanford University.

## 3. The mathematics, plainly

The paper's section 3 sets up three definitions, and everything else is a discretisation of the third.

Definition 1 is self-attention as everyone writes it: Att(X) = softmax(XW_q (XW_k)ᵀ / √d) XW_v, with three d × d matrices. The paper then observes that "the self-attention can be viewed as a kernel summation," Att(X)[s] = Σ_t X[t] κ[s,t], with an asymmetric matrix-valued kernel κ[s,t] = K[s,t] · W_v. So attention is a sum over tokens t, for each token s, weighted by a kernel that depends on both.

Definition 2 makes that continuous. A kernel integral operator on a domain D is K(X)(s) = ∫_D κ(s,t) X(t) dt for all s in D, with κ a continuous kernel from D × D to d × d matrices. Attention is one discretisation of this with a kernel that depends on the input. Definition 3 restricts the kernel to depend only on the difference: K(X)(s) = ∫_D κ(s − t) X(t) dt. That is a global convolution, and the convolution theorem says it is a pointwise multiplication in the Fourier domain. Because the definition is on a continuous domain, the operator does not know the grid, which is the property the paper calls resolution independence: "After training on one resolution it can be directly evaluated at another resolution (zero-shot super-resolution)."

The FNO is the discrete form in three steps, and the paper writes them as token mixing z_m,n = [DFT(X)]_m,n, channel mixing z̃_m,n = W_m,n z_m,n, and token demixing y_m,n = [IDFT(Z̃)]_m,n. The indices m, n run over the two spatial frequencies of an image. W_m,n is a d × d complex matrix, one per frequency, which is where the O(Nd²) parameters come from.

AFNO changes step two three ways. First, W_m,n is made block diagonal with k blocks, each (d/k) × (d/k), so z̃⁽ˡ⁾_m,n = W⁽ˡ⁾_m,n z⁽ˡ⁾_m,n for ℓ = 1, …, k. The paper says the blocks are "interpretable as heads in multi-head self-attention." Second, the per-frequency matrices are dropped and one two-layer perceptron is used at every frequency: z̃_m,n = MLP(z_m,n) = W₂ σ(W₁ z_m,n) + b, and "the weights W₁, W₂, b are shared for all tokens." Because the MLP is applied after the DFT, its input at each frequency is different, so the effective filter depends on the input; that is the sense of "adaptive." Third, the output is passed through a soft threshold, S_λ(x) = sign(x) max{|x| − λ, 0}, which the paper derives as the closed-form solution of a LASSO problem, min ‖z̃_m,n − W_m,n z_m,n‖² + λ‖z̃_m,n‖₁. Coefficients smaller than λ become zero; the rest move toward zero by λ.

The paper's Figure 3 gives the whole mixer as eight lines: take a real 2D FFT of the token grid, reshape the channel axis into k blocks, apply the block MLP, reshape back, soft-shrink, inverse real FFT, add the input back as a residual. The paper also notes that GFN, a prior Fourier mixer, "is a special case of FNO, when W_m,n is diagonal and channels are separable," so the three mixers sit on one line: diagonal, block diagonal, full.

## 4. Its experiments and numbers as reported

The paper's Table 1 gives the cost of each mixer in FLOPs and parameters for N tokens of d channels.

| mixer | FLOPs | parameters | the paper's label |
|---|---|---|---|
| self-attention | N²d + 3Nd² | 3d² | graph global convolution |
| GFN | Nd + Nd log N | Nd | depthwise global convolution |
| FNO | Nd² + Nd log N | Nd² | global convolution |
| AFNO | Nd²/k + Nd log N | (1 + 4/k)d² + 4d | adaptive global convolution |

The experiments are four. The first is inpainting on ImageNet-1k at 224 × 224 with a ViT-B/4 backbone, every model at 87M parameters, in Table 2. Self-attention gives SSIM 0.931 and PSNR 27.06 dB at 357.2 GFLOPs and 1.2 s latency. AFNO gives 0.931 and 27.05 dB at 257.2 GFLOPs and 0.8 s. GFN gives 0.928 and 26.76 dB at 177.8 GFLOPs and 0.7 s. LS, the Long-Short transformer, gives 0.920 and 26.18 dB at 274.2 GFLOPs and 1.4 s. The paper's sentence is that "AFNO matches the performance of Self-Attention despite using significantly less FLOPs."

The second is few-shot segmentation after that inpainting pretraining, in Table 3, with the same backbones. On LSUN-Cats the scores are self-attention 35.57, LS 20.29, GFN 34.52, AFNO 35.73. On ADE-Cars, 49.26, 29.66, 47.84, 49.60. On CelebA-Faces, 56.91, 41.36, 55.21, 55.75. The paper says "AFNO surpasses Self-Attention for 2/3 datasets while using less flops."

The third is Cityscapes semantic segmentation at 1024 × 1024 with a SegFormer-B3 backbone of 45M parameters, in Table 4. Efficient self-attention reaches 79.7 mIoU at 380.7 total GFLOPs, of which 129.9 are the mixer. LS reaches 80.5 at 409.1 and 85.0. GFN reaches 80.4 at 363.4 and 2.6. AFNO with all frequencies kept reaches 80.9 at 440.0 and 23.7. AFNO keeping only 25% of the low frequencies reaches 80.4 at 429.0 and 12.4. The paper says the 25% variant "only loses 0.05 mIoU," which does not agree with the two mIoU values as extracted; we report both as extracted and do not resolve it. Appendix A.3 says the backbone was pretrained on ImageNet-1k classification for 300 epochs with Adam, learning rate 10⁻³ and batch size 1024. Figure 1 says that across SegFormer-B0 to B3 "AFNO consistently outperforms other mixers" on the parameter-count against mIoU trade-off.

The fourth is ImageNet-1k classification with a ViT-S/4 backbone of 16M parameters, in Table 5: LS 80.87 top-1 and 95.31 top-5 at 15.8 GFLOPs, GFN 78.77 and 94.4 at 6.1, AFNO 80.89 and 95.39 at 15.3.

The ablations are in Figure 4 and Table 6. Varying λ from 0 to 10, the best result is at λ = 0.01, and hard thresholding that removes 65% of the frequencies degrades performance. Varying k from 1 to 64 at constant parameter count, k = 8 gives the best PSNR. Table 6, on a ViT-XS/4 of 16M parameters with 10% of ImageNet for pretraining and CelebA-Faces for finetuning, gives FNO 24.8 PSNR and 39.27 mIoU, AFNO with non-adaptive weights 25.1 and 44.04, AFNO with hard thresholding at 35% 23.58 and 34.17, and full AFNO 25.69 and 49.49. The paper's sentence is that "adaptive weights are crucial to AFNO's performance."

## 5. The one idea, restated

Strip the paper to one sentence and it is this: a global convolution in the Fourier domain is a token mixer, and to make it a good one you have to decide what to do at each frequency with less than a full matrix.

The FNO's answer was a full complex matrix at every retained frequency and nothing at the rest. That is a hard threshold on frequency and no threshold on the coefficient. It is the right answer for a PDE on a smooth field with a few thousand grid points and it is the wrong answer for an image with 65k pixels, where the parameter count grows with the grid and the weights cannot look at the input.

AFNO's answer has three parts, and each part removes one freedom the FNO had. The blocks remove the freedom for every channel to mix with every other channel. The shared MLP removes the freedom for every frequency to have its own weights, and puts back a smaller freedom, that the weights may depend on the coefficient they see. The soft threshold removes the freedom for a small coefficient to survive. What is left is a mixer with (1 + 4/k)d² + 4d parameters whatever the resolution, a cost of Nd²/k + Nd log N, and an output that is sparse in frequency in the way the paper says images already are.

The paper's own evidence for the idea is Table 6. Going from FNO to AFNO with static weights buys 0.3 dB and 4.8 mIoU on the small model; letting the weights adapt buys another 0.6 dB and 5.5 mIoU; replacing the soft threshold with a hard one loses 2.1 dB and 15.3 mIoU. So the idea, in the paper's own ranking, is that the shrinkage matters most, the adaptation second, the blocks third. The paper's ablation on k found k = 8 best, which says the blocks are not free either: k = 1 is the full matrix and k = 64 is the diagonal, and the paper's best sits between.

Phical, read against this, is an operator over a function with 32–64 coordinates. It has never had to decide what to do at each frequency with less than a full matrix, because its function is small enough that a full matrix is 8,360 parameters at hinge and 50,016 at wide+deep, both of which fit. The paper's idea is not a way to make phinet cheaper. It is a list of the three freedoms an operator over a function can give up, with a measurement of what each is worth on one kind of data. Sections 6 to 10 ask, for each, whether Phical has already given it up without meaning to.

## 6. The token as a word, the image as a context

The paper's input is a grid of tokens, h × w positions each carrying d channels, and its mixer moves information between positions. In the ViT-B/4 experiments a 224 × 224 image at patch size 4 is 56 × 56 = 3,136 tokens; in the Cityscapes experiment the paper says the sequence is 65k. The channel dimension d is the smaller axis, and it is the one the paper cuts into blocks.

Phical's Tensor is [b, w, s], complex. b is the batch. w is words. s is channels, 32–64 of them, each a complex phasor on a closed circle with a count. So there is a direct reading of the paper's grid: a token is a word, a token's d channels are a word's s relation channels, and the sequence of tokens is a text. Under that reading the paper's mixer is an operator that moves a word's relations toward what the surrounding words demand, which is what "under what conditions (c)" means when the condition is a context.

The reading is exact on the channel axis and unmeasured on the sequence axis. On the channel axis the numbers already line up: d is 64 in Phical and the paper's ablation cuts d into k = 8 blocks of d/k, so a Phical word under AFNO would be 8 blocks of 8 channels. The helix reads the same 64 channels as codons, 64 = base-4 ACGT of the channel, and the sphere tie reads them as 64 = 8² spherical harmonics Y_l^m with l < 8. That three readings of one axis agree on the number 8 is arithmetic, not a result; we note it because the paper's k = 8 was found by ablation on images and Phical's 8 was chosen.

On the sequence axis nothing is measured. The facts file does not say which axis phinet's unitary FFT runs on, and we do not claim it. What the facts file does say is that the operator on Austen was trained on 204,592 examples from a pool of 1,228 words seen 20 or more times, and that it moves one word's function toward the next. That is a mixer over a sequence of length two, X and its successor. The paper's mixer takes the whole grid at once. So the paper's question, put to phiano, is whether a text of w words, each 64 channels, should be mixed in the Fourier domain along w. The decoding numbers say something about why one might want that. On 40 held-out prompts × 30 tokens the operator nearest-k sampler has Kneser-Ney perplexity 66.9 against the trigram sampler's 13.3, and shares 8.7% of its 4-grams across prompts against the sampler's 25.7%. The operator is less repetitive and less fluent. A mixer that sees more than one preceding word is the obvious thing to try, and this paper says how to do it at N log N.

What the token is not, in Phical, is a patch. The paper's token stands for 4 × 4 pixels and its channels are learned. A Phical word's channels are relations under conditions, 32–64 of them, each with a meaning fixed by the quest kind that trained it. The paper's blocks are heads with no names. Phical's blocks, if it had them, would have names.

## 7. The block as a kind, the channel as a coordinate

The paper's first change cuts the d × d channel-mixing matrix into k diagonal blocks. Inside a block, channels mix. Across blocks, they do not. The paper offers the blocks as heads and reports that k = 8 is best at fixed parameters, k = 1 (the full matrix) and k = 64 (the diagonal) both worse.

Phical has a stronger form of this already, and it is measured. In the quest, the phiecs synthetic oracle has 256 words in families of 8 and five kinds of relation, is-a, part-of, near, used-for, unlike, in 1,280 rows against 293 target words, chance MRR 0.021. The attraction-only trainer with dithered rounding reaches 0.594 MRR, hit@1 34.8%, hit@5 97.4%. Within kind the figure is 0.747. Read on the wrong channel it is 0.015, below chance. Read at an offset it is 0.005. So a relation lives on its channel and on no other. A trainer that let every channel mix with every other would have to unlearn that, and the wrong-channel number says the shipped trainer never learned it in the first place.

That is a block-diagonal structure with k equal to the number of channels, which is the paper's k = 64 case, the diagonal, the one the paper calls GFN and finds worse than k = 8. The paper's argument for k = 8 is that some channels should mix. Its argument against k = 1 is that not all should. Phical's channel is a coordinate with a name, and the question the paper puts is which named coordinates ought to share a block.

The kinds give one answer. The five kinds are five conditions, and a block per kind would let the channels trained under is-a mix among themselves and not with the channels trained under unlike. The held-out numbers give a reason to want that. On the held-out 20%, one hop is 0.011 and two hops reach 0.095 on is-a and 0.093 on used-for, with part-of, near and unlike at about chance. Two hops is a composition, a word reached through another word on the same kind. A block that mixes the is-a channels could in principle learn that composition in one step. Whether it would is not measured.

The sphere tie gives a second answer and it is measured. phinet::Tie::Sphere reads the 64 channels as Y_l^m with l < 8 and puts one complex weight per degree l. That is a block structure by degree, 8 blocks of sizes 1, 3, 5, …, 15, with one weight per block rather than a full block matrix. It has 1,192 parameters against the circle tie's 8,360 and reaches 12.8× chance valid and 13.1× test on Austen hinge at 3 epochs, against the circle's 14.7× and 14.6×, in 89 s against 142 s. On the synthetic degree-filter task, 200 items, 40 epochs, the sphere reaches MRR 1.000 where the circle reaches 0.662 and identity 0.646. So a tie whose blocks match the structure of the task wins the task outright and loses a little on Austen, where nothing says the structure is by degree. That is the paper's Table 6 read on a different function: structure is worth what the data's structure is worth.

## 8. The shared MLP as X ⇒ Y under c

The paper's second change is the one it calls adaptive. Instead of a matrix W_m,n at each frequency, one two-layer perceptron W₂ σ(W₁ z) + b is applied to the coefficient at every frequency. Because the input differs at each frequency, so does the effective linear map, and because the MLP is shared, the parameter count no longer depends on N. Table 6 says the adaptation is worth 0.6 dB and 5.5 mIoU on the small model.

In Phical terms the change is this. The FNO's W_m,n is an operator X ⇒ Y whose form is fixed by the frequency and does not depend on what arrives. AFNO's MLP is an operator whose form depends on what arrives. The paradigm says meaning is a side effect of pairs of words moving under a condition, and "under a condition" is the phrase that the FNO does not have and AFNO does. A static filter moves every word the same way. An adaptive one moves a word according to the coefficient it sees, which is to say according to the word.

Phinet has measured operators of both kinds on Austen, and the facts file names them by their loss and shape rather than by adaptivity. Identity reaches 7.9× chance. The hinge operator, 8,360 parameters, reaches 14.7× valid and 14.6× test. Softmax at β = 8 reaches 18.0× and 18.8×. Softmax wide+deep, 50,016 parameters, reaches 27.6× at 3 epochs and 30.7× valid, 30.3× test at 8 epochs, with hit@10 0.348. Wide+deep is the only operator in that list with more than one layer, and the paper's adaptive filter is exactly a two-layer map in the coefficient. Whether phinet's wide+deep is adaptive in the paper's sense, a nonlinearity applied per mode after the FFT, is not stated in the facts file and we do not claim it. What the numbers say is that going from a linear operator to a deeper one doubled the score on the same data, and that the paper found the same direction on images.

There is a second place the paper's adaptivity lands, and it is the persona. Phiano's personas are conditions. On three writers, Chesterton, Austen and Carroll, held-out 4-sentence paragraphs are attributed at 71% overall, 74, 65 and 86 by writer, against 33% chance. In dialogue, 18 replies are judged as their persona 44% by a sector reader and 72% by a Kneser-Ney panel. A persona is a c under which the same X ⇒ Y moves differently. The paper's MLP makes c the coefficient itself. Phiano's persona makes c a label. The two are the same slot filled from two sides, the input's own content on one and an external condition on the other, and the paper's evidence is that filling it from the input's side is worth more than anything else in the mixer.

The cost is that an adaptive filter is no longer a convolution. Definition 3 needs κ(s − t), one kernel for all positions, and that is what makes the operator resolution-independent. An MLP after the FFT is still one map at every frequency, so the resolution argument survives, but the result is no longer the Fourier transform of a fixed kernel. The paper does not say so in those words. It keeps the name operator and adds the word adaptive.

## 9. The soft threshold as the deadband, and the helix

The paper's third change is the soft threshold, S_λ(x) = sign(x) max{|x| − λ, 0}, applied to every Fourier coefficient after the MLP. Coefficients below λ vanish. The rest shrink by λ. The paper found λ = 0.01 best and found hard thresholding, which keeps a fixed fraction of low frequencies and drops the rest, clearly worse: 23.58 dB and 34.17 mIoU against 25.69 and 49.49 in Table 6.

Phinet's Fno has the hard threshold. The profile records FnoConfig { space, width, layers, modes, margin, lr }, and modes is the number of low frequencies kept, which is the FNO's original rule and the paper's worst row. Phinet has never run a soft threshold. That is the experiment in section 12.

But Phient has a threshold of another kind that it did not choose, and it has measured what it costs. A channel is one byte, 256 quanta of 2π. The trainer's nudge is 0.06 · sin d, where d is the angular distance to the target. Once sin d < 0.2, about 11°, the nudge is under half a quantum and nearest rounding returns the phasor to where it was. Nearest rounding froze every pair 8 quanta short of its target. That is a threshold on the update rather than on the coefficient: a move smaller than λ is zero, and a move larger than λ lands exactly, which is closer to hard than to soft. The paper's soft threshold would instead let the small move accumulate and take λ off the large one.

The fix Phient shipped for the freeze is dither, rounding with noise, and it has a measured cost: corpus attribution falls from 71% to 65% with dither on, so it ships off by default. The quest number of 0.594 MRR is the attraction-only trainer with dithered rounding; the shipped trainer without it reaches 0.082. So the deadband is worth a factor of seven on the quest and costs six points on attribution, and the two settings ship differently. The paper's λ sweep in Figure 4 is the same trade run on images with a smoother knob, and the paper's answer was that a small nonzero λ beats both zero and a hard cut. Phient's knob is one bit, dither or not, and the facts file measures the two ends.

The helix is where the threshold's output would live. The lexicon is a DNA-coded tree: 64 codons as base-4 ACGT of the channel, a hexagram, a sphere degree and order, B-DNA xyz at 10.5 codons per turn and 0.34 nm rise, a PhiTon wavelength, band and hex per codon. 18,496 words are written in 1.9 s to 75 MB with a byte-exact round trip. Byte-exact is the point. A soft-thresholded coefficient is a real number shrunk by λ, and the helix stores a byte. Any shrinkage phinet applied in the frequency domain would have to survive the round trip through 256 quanta, and the deadband says a shrinkage of less than half a quantum would not. The paper's λ = 0.01 is, on a unit circle, about 0.4 quanta. We do not claim the two scales are comparable; the paper's coefficients are not phasors on a byte. We note that the paper's best λ is at the scale where Phient's rounding already decides.

## 10. The mixer in phiecs, the quest and the agent loop

The paper's mixer is one layer inside a transformer block, and the block is applied L times. Each application mixes tokens globally, then mixes channels locally with an ordinary MLP, and the residual carries the input past both. The paper's Figure 3 puts the residual inside the AFNO call: return x + bias, where bias is the input.

Phiecs is an entity-component world, and the quest is its loop. A word is an entity. Its 32–64 channels are components. The quest picks a pair of words and a kind, and the trainer moves the source word's phasor on that kind's channel toward the target's, by 0.06 · sin d, and rounds. Then it picks the next pair. That is the paper's mixer with a sequence of length two, one channel active, and no FFT. The residual is the rounding: a word that is not moved stays byte-exact where it was.

What the paper adds to that loop is the global step. Its mixer sees every token at once and moves each according to all. The quest sees one pair at a time and moves one. The held-out numbers say what that costs. One hop on held-out rows is 0.011 MRR against chance of 0.021. The trainer has learned nothing about a pair it has not seen, even when both words were seen in other pairs. Two hops reaches 0.095 on is-a and 0.093 on used-for, so the structure is there for the composing kinds and the loop does not compose it. A global mixer along the family axis, 8 words per family, would see every member at once. The paper's cost for that is Nd²/k + Nd log N with N = 8, which is nothing. The engine is allocation and bandwidth-bound at these sizes, a 1 MB op at 2–5 ms, so the question is not cost. It is whether a Fourier transform along a family of 8 words means anything, and that is not measured.

The agent loop in phiano is generation. Forty held-out prompts × 30 tokens, and the operator nearest-k sampler chooses each next word by moving the current word's function and reading the nearest. Its perplexity is 66.9 under Kneser-Ney, down from 97.2 in an earlier run, against the trigram sampler's 13.3, and the union of the two is 66.8. The paper's block would replace "move the current word's function" with "mix the last w words' functions in the Fourier domain along w, then read." The paper's resolution argument says that mixer would be the same operator at w = 8 as at w = 64, which is a property a context window has never had. Whether it would move the perplexity is not measured, and the union number says that simply adding the operator's votes to the sampler's did not.

The sphere is the last landing. The paper's DFT is on a flat grid. The profile records that later FourCastNet versions moved to spherical harmonics to respect the globe, and phinet's Tie::Sphere already reads 64 channels as Y_l^m. So phinet's Fourier axis is, in one tie, already the sphere, and the paper's mixer on that tie would be a block MLP per degree with a soft threshold on the harmonic coefficients. That is one line of code away from the sphere tie's current one weight per degree and has not been written.

| diagonal, k = d | quest, wrong channel vs within kind | 0.015 vs 0.747 |
| adaptive, deeper filter | softmax wide+deep, 50,016 params, 8 epochs | 30.7× / 30.3×, hit@10 0.348 |
| linear filter | softmax β = 8 | 18.0× / 18.8× |
| threshold on update | byte deadband, nearest rounding | froze every pair 8 quanta short |
| threshold removed | dither on: quest; attribution | 0.594 vs 0.082 MRR; 71% → 65% |
| resolution independence | none | not measured |
| global mixing over tokens | none | not measured |

The first row is the plain FNO, one complex weight vector on the 64 channels, no blocks, no adaptivity, no threshold beyond modes. Its 14.7× against chance MRR 0.0063 is the baseline any AFNO variant in phinet would have to beat, and the identity operator's 7.9× is the floor.

The second and third rows are the closest Phient has to the paper's block ablation. The sphere tie has one seventh of the circle tie's parameters and loses 1.9× on Austen valid, and wins the degree-filter task outright. The paper's Figure 4 found k = 8 best on images at constant parameters; the sphere tie is not at constant parameters and its 8 blocks are of unequal size, so it is a different point on a different curve.

The fourth row is Phical's channel discipline. 0.747 within kind against 0.015 on the wrong channel is 50 to 1, and it means the quest's trained relations are diagonal in the channel basis. The paper found the diagonal worse than k = 8 on images. The quest's held-out numbers, 0.011 at one hop, are consistent with a diagonal that cannot compose, and are also consistent with other causes; the facts file does not separate them.

The fifth and sixth rows are the nearest thing to the paper's static-versus-adaptive ablation, and they are confounded by parameter count, 50,016 against the hinge's 8,360, and by loss. The facts file records the sphere tie as unrun at softmax and wide+deep, so the block-and-adaptive cell in the paper's grid is empty in Phient.

The seventh and eighth rows are the deadband. They are the only measurements in the family that bear on the paper's soft threshold, and they bear on it from the update side rather than the coefficient side.

The last two rows are empty and are the reason for section 12. Phient has also done no weather work, no Phi-4 run, and has no partnership with anyone; the vision experiments in the paper have no counterpart in gemphi at all.

## 12. The experiment that would test the paper's idea inside phinet

The test is the paper's Table 6 run on Austen, one row at a time, against the circle tie's hinge baseline.

The data is fixed: Emma, Persuasion and Sense and Sensibility, the pool of 1,228 words seen 20 or more times, 204,592 training examples, chance MRR 0.0063, the valid and test splits that gave 14.7× and 14.6×. The metric is MRR as a multiple of chance and hit@10, both already reported for the baseline. The loss is hinge, because that is the loss the baseline has, and the epoch count is 3 so that the sphere tie's 3-epoch figures are comparable.

The control is the existing circle tie, 8,360 parameters, 14.7× valid, 14.6× test, 142 s. Every variant is compared to it and to identity at 7.9×, and any variant that does not beat identity is reported as such.

Variant one is the soft threshold alone. After phinet's unitary FFT and the tie's multiply, apply S_λ to each complex coefficient, on its modulus, and inverse-transform. Sweep λ over the paper's range, 0 to 10, on a log scale, with λ = 0 as a second control that should reproduce the baseline exactly. Report the best λ and the score at λ = 0.01, the paper's best. Also run the hard threshold at 25% and 35% of modes, which is FnoConfig's existing modes knob, so the paper's soft-versus-hard row exists in Phient.

Variant two is the block structure alone. Replace the circle tie's per-channel weights with k block matrices of size (64/k)², k in {1, 4, 8, 16, 64}, and hold the total parameter count near 8,360 by the paper's rule, (1 + 4/k)d² + 4d, or by padding. k = 64 is the diagonal and should reproduce the baseline within noise. k = 8 is the paper's best. Report the curve. Then run the same k on the sphere tie so that blocks by degree and blocks by index are both on the page.

Variant three is the adaptive filter alone. Replace the per-mode weight with one two-layer complex MLP, W₂ σ(W₁ z) + b, shared across modes, at the parameter counts of hinge and of wide+deep, 8,360 and 50,016, so that the comparison to 14.7× and to 30.7× is at equal size. The nonlinearity must be chosen for complex input and stated.

Variant four is all three together at k = 8 and the best λ, which is AFNO. Its result is the paper's claim measured in phinet.

Two further things are reported whatever the scores. First, wall time against the baseline's 142 s, because the paper's claim is an efficiency claim and phinet's engine is bandwidth-bound at 2–5 ms per 1 MB op, so any FLOP saving may not show. Second, the byte round trip: every variant's output phasors are written through the helix and read back, and any coefficient the soft threshold shrank below half a quantum is counted, so the deadband's interaction with λ is a number and not a paragraph. The one thing this experiment cannot test is resolution independence, because Phical's function has 64 coordinates at training and at test; a run at 32 channels evaluated at 64 would test it and is not designed here.

## 13. Where we disagree or where the analogy breaks

The analogy breaks first on size. The paper's whole argument is about N, the number of tokens, being large: 3,136 on ImageNet at patch 4, 65k on Cityscapes. Its three changes are there to keep parameters and FLOPs from growing with N. Phical's function has 32–64 coordinates. Its full operator at hinge is 8,360 parameters and at wide+deep 50,016, and the engine is bandwidth-bound at these sizes. Nothing the paper saves is a cost Phient pays. If the paper's changes help phinet, it will be for the reason in Table 6, generalisation, and not for the reason in Table 1.

It breaks second on what the Fourier axis is. The paper's DFT runs over positions in an image, and it is a global convolution because an image's statistics are shift-invariant enough for that to be a sensible prior. A Phical word's 64 channels are relation kinds, and a shift along the channel axis is not a symmetry of anything; channel 3 under is-a and channel 4 under part-of are not neighbours. The sphere tie gets around this by reading the channels as harmonics, where the transform is between the channel index and the degree, and that is a change of basis rather than a convolution over a sequence. The facts file does not state which axis phinet's FFT runs on. Until it does, the honest statement is that the paper's mixer and phinet's Fno share the FFT and the learned complex weights and need not share the thing being transformed.

It breaks third on sparsity. The paper says images are sparse in the Fourier domain with the energy at low frequencies, and its soft threshold and its 25% variant lean on that. Whether a word's relation circle is sparse in any Fourier basis is not measured. The degree-filter task is synthetic and was built to be sparse by degree; the sphere's 1.000 there says the tie can find sparsity that was put in, not that Austen has it. The circle tie beating the sphere tie on Austen, 14.7× to 12.8×, is weak evidence the other way.

We disagree on one point of language. The paper keeps the name operator for a mixer whose channel map is an input-dependent MLP. Definition 3, the one that gives resolution independence, is a convolution with a fixed kernel, and an adaptive filter is not that. The paper is careful to derive resolution independence from the continuous formulation and then to say the MLP is shared across tokens, which preserves the property that the parameter count is free of N. It does not say that the resulting map is a convolution, and it is not. This matters for Phient because the paradigm's X ⇒ Y under c is precisely a map that depends on c, and the paper's adaptive step is where the operator view and the conditional view part. We would call AFNO a conditional operator and say so.

Last, the paper's deadband is chosen and Phient's is inherited. The paper swept λ and picked 0.01. Phient's threshold is half a byte quantum and came from the storage format, and the only knob is dither, which helped the quest seven-fold and cost attribution six points. That is not a disagreement with the paper. It is an admission that the paper studied a knob Phient has and has not turned.

## 14. The person and the moment

Everything here is from the profile in the parent folder, and nothing about her intentions toward Phient is claimed.

The paper was submitted to arXiv on 24 November 2021 and published at ICLR 2022. By then she had been at NVIDIA as senior director of machine-learning research since 2018, having opened its core AI/ML lab in Santa Clara, and Bren Professor at Caltech since the same year, where she co-founded the AI4Science initiative. The paper's affiliations, NVIDIA, Caltech and Stanford, are those two institutions and a third, and the last author is Bryan Catanzaro of NVIDIA. Zongyi Li, third author here, was first author of the Fourier Neural Operator paper of 2020 that this one adapts.

The paper sits between two of her group's results. Behind it is the FNO, which did the global integral kernel of a PDE as a multiplication in the frequency domain and was, for turbulent flows, orders of magnitude faster than the solvers it learned from; Quanta profiled it in 2021. Ahead of it, three months later, is FourCastNet, February 2022, which the profile records as putting an adaptive FNO on the whole planet: a 720 × 1440 grid at 0.25°, 20 prognostic variables, a week's forecast in under two seconds, roughly 45,000× faster than numerical weather prediction on a node-hour basis. The word adaptive in FourCastNet's title is this paper's. So the paper is the bridge from a PDE solver to a weather model, and the bridge was built through vision, on ImageNet and Cityscapes, because a high-resolution image and a global weather state are both grids too large for attention.

Her own account of her method, in the profile's words from the Latent Space interview, is "All of the things that work with deep learning, let's take them, but make them a bit more principled." This paper is that sentence as a design. It takes the transformer block, which works, and replaces one part of it with an operator, which is principled, and then makes three changes to the operator that are each argued from a property of images. Her later view, that "we have foundation models for language, not for physics" and that transformers cannot be the substrate for physics at scale because a few hundred grid points per dimension is a context of hundreds of billions of tokens, is the same argument as the paper's introduction with the numbers made larger.

The profile lists, after this paper, the ACM Fellowship in 2022, a NeurIPS Outstanding Paper in 2022, the Guggenheim and Schmidt AI2050 fellowships in 2023, the Blavatnik Award in 2024, the IEEE Kiyo Tomiyasu Award and Time100 Impact Award in 2025, and the UN Scientific Advisory Board on 16 March 2026. The moment of this paper is before all of that, in the winter she and her group were learning that an operator built for smooth fields could be made to work on data with edges, if one was willing to shrink what was small.

## 15. What remains

The experiment in section 12 has not been run. Until it is, everything in sections 6 to 10 is a reading, and the only measured statements are the ones in section 11, all made for other reasons.

Three things would have to be settled before the experiment means what it should. The first is the axis. The facts file does not say whether phinet's unitary FFT runs over the channel axis, the word axis or both, and the paper's mixer is defined by the axis it transforms. The second is the complex nonlinearity for variant three; the paper's σ is a ReLU on real coefficients from a real FFT, and phinet's Tensor is complex. The third is the parameter-matching rule for the block sweep, so that the k curve is at constant size the way the paper's is.

Two things the paper does that Phient cannot yet do at all. Resolution independence needs a function that can be sampled at two resolutions, and a Phical word has a fixed 32–64 channels. Whether a word trained at 32 channels and read at 64 is a meaningful question is itself open. And global mixing over a context needs a phiano that presents more than one preceding word to the operator, which the decoding runs, at one word moved toward the next, do not.

One thing the paper does not do that Phient must. The paper's coefficients are floats. Phient's are bytes, 256 quanta of 2π, and every result in the family has passed through that rounding. The soft threshold at the paper's best λ is at the scale of that quantum. Whether shrinkage and rounding compose, cancel or fight is the most Phient-specific question this paper raises, and the section 12 design counts it.

Not claimed: any weather result, since Phient has done no weather work; any Phi-4 result, since only the synthetic oracle has been run; the sphere tie at softmax or wide+deep, which is unrun; and any endorsement by the author. The next paper in this series is on the model this one made possible.

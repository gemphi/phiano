# 138 · Kin: the unheard word read through the words told the same company

In Phient's world, a word (w) is a seed: what it relates to, under what conditions (c), grows from it. The Phical paradigm, X ⇒ Y, drives and composes Phient's Phasor Language Models: Phinet, Phiano, PhiTon. Phical stores no point: a word is 32–64 relations, each a complex phasor on a closed circle with a count; meaning is a side effect of pairs of words moving under a condition.

docs/137 left one stratum open: the 23% of a new paragraph's positions where the word that came was never told after the last word or the last pair, where the counts fall back to the prior and the operator reached no further. This doc tries the paradigm's own answer to it, with counts alone and no epochs: a word's reading is its own plus its *kin's* (docs/97's inheritance, `own ⊕ λ · parents`, with the parents found rather than declared). Measured on the same held-out set; the result is a second honest no, and it says more precisely what the open stratum is.

## 1. Kin

Two words are kin to the degree they were told the same company (Wittgenstein §67: not one thing in common, but a family of resemblances): the same words before them — the pairs each closes, `pair:u w`, which every word says under `says` — and the same words after them, its `next` table. Kinship is the mean of the two cosines between those count tables (`Counts::kin`, `relate/kin.rs`), found once per learning from posting lists over the tree's words (scopes, pairs and `scope|word` conditions have `next` tables too and are not kin). Nothing is declared: a word told once has the kin its one sentence gives it, a word never told has none. The kin found are what one would hope — `said`: replied 0.87, cried 0.84, exclaimed 0.78; `walked`: hurried 0.61, went 0.55, hesitated 0.50; `of`: in, by, on; `very`: too, so, most; `emma`: harriet 0.89 — and then thousands more at lower kinship, and among the near ones also `therefore` and `weariness` beside `emma`, two sparse tables that happen to share a comma.

The kin reading of a word is the mixture of its kin's `next` distributions, each smoothed toward the prior with weight one and weighted by kinship (`Counts::kin_base`, passed sparsely, kept per word). `Knowing.kin` makes it the base the levels move from, in place of the prior: the word's own counts and the pair's then move it (D4 nested). `story --held-out … --kin` adds the rows and the column below. The kin of 2,500 distinct last words and their readings take about four minutes over 10,578 positions on one core.

## 2. Measured

Same held-out set (5% of *Emma*, 101 paragraphs, 10,578 positions, seed 7), same strata as docs/137.

Read plainly. Where the count has heard the word go on, the kin reading alone — no epochs, no operator — reaches 14.2% top-1, above the operator's 11.5% after 25 minutes of training and twice the prior: kin carry real information about what a word goes on to, and they carry it the moment the words are told. Where the count has heard nothing, the kin reach *less* far than the prior (0.5% against 3.4%), and as a base they cost perplexity (159 → 165 flat). The reason is in the mixture: the last word in this stratum is usually rare, its kin by cosine are other rare words — two tables of two entries that share a comma — and a rare kin's `next` table, divided by its small total, puts its whole weight on the one or two words it happened to be followed by. Six thousand such kin do not average to the language; they average to noise sharper than the prior. The near kin are right and few; the far kin are many and wrong, and kinship by cosine does not know where the one ends and the other begins.

## 3. What the open stratum is

Two attempts at it now, both below the prior: the operator trained on `word next word` (docs/137) and the kin mixture (this doc). Before guessing why, the stratum was counted (a throwaway reading of the same held-out set). Of its 2,431 positions, the word that came is among the prior's twenty commonest in 483 (20%) and among its hundred commonest in 919 (38%); in 1,512 (62%) it is a word outside the hundred. The last word was heard three times or fewer in only 462 (19%). So the stratum is not, for the most part, a rare word before a comma: four times in five the last word is well known, and three times in five the word that came is an ordinary content word it was never followed by — `her`, `in`, `with` are the commonest truths after the punctuation, and after them the long tail of the novel. That *is* the territory the paradigm's claim is about: a relation never told, reachable only through what else is known about the two words. The prior's 3.4% is the 20% of commas and `the`s; the other 80% nothing here reads at all, and the kin by cosine and the operator by hinge both fail there in the same way — each finds the shape of a continuation, neither the word.

The test is therefore the right one, and the reading that passes it is still to be built. What it has to do is now specific: for a known word followed by a word it never was, rank that word among the thousands by what the two words' companies share — and do it with the near kin alone, since the far kin are what the mixture drowned in.

What stands: the kin are found from what was told and are good where they are near (14.2% where the counts also know, twice the prior, no epochs); `Counts::kin` is the first reading in the tree that is neither a count of this word nor a trained function, and the one to build the inference on — with the far kin cut by a rule the measurements have to earn (the D2 post, perhaps: a kin heard only when its distribution moves the prior by τ), not by a knob set today.

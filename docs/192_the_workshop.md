# 192 · The workshop — teacher, student and compiler coupled on the English guide

In Phient's world, a word (w) is a seed: what it relates to, under what conditions (c), grows from it. The Phical paradigm, X ⇒ Y, drives and composes Phient's Phasor Language Models: Phinet, Phiano, PhiTon. Phical stores no point: a word is 32–64 relations, each a complex phasor on a closed circle with a count; meaning is a side effect of pairs of words moving under a condition.

## The assumption, stated

A model learning a language needs two different helpers: a TEACHER for what is use — which words go with which, in which construction — and a COMPILER for what is logic — counting, conditions, procedure — things not related to memorising. Phiano's phasors hold the first (docs/187–190); phicpl holds the second (docs/178): a deterministic judge that answers in microseconds. The guide — English Grammar in Use, 129 units parsed from the book's own text — is the curriculum for both.

## The workflow, per unit

1. BROUGHT: before the unit is told, the student predicts every next word of the unit's sentences — what earlier lessons carried in. 2. TOLD: the unit's sentences are told to the phasors, on the fly. 3. UNSURE: the student names the unit's constructions it has seen once — the quest prompts it sends a teacher (family_prompt; ollama on the machine, Spawned::ollama). 4. WORKSHOP: the unit's own numbered exercises are the teacher's workshop prompts. 5. COMPILER: one task that is logic — here, count the negatives the student read among the unit's sentences — is written as BCPL from the student's own flags, run by phicpl, and judged against the student's count. 6. PAGE: everything above is written to the page as it happened.

## The pages

docs/193_workshop.md — 65 pages for 129 units, two units a page, generated by examples/workshop.rs in fifty seconds; nothing on them is written by hand. Across the whole course the student brought 11.6% of each unit's next words before being told it (14,697 of 126,259) — what it carried from every earlier lesson into the next, with nothing retrained; the per-unit figure climbs from 0.0% on Unit 1 to ~11% by the passives. Every compiler task judged equal to the student's count (e.g. Unit 1: 12 = 12, 2,388 O-code steps).

## Talking (docs/192 also ships examples/talk.rs)

The whole book told to the phasors in 3.6 s, then questions as conditions: i have been waiting → "for the job"; if i had known → "i wish"; when do we use the present perfect → "continuous past"; she has been working → "for". The first words are the author's; the voice then slides toward function words where the deep channel is silent — the prose gap of docs/190, unchanged by scoring agreement over amplitude (tried: arena 32.8%, no gain; kept as the paradigm-truer read).
